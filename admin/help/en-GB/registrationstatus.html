Tickets

Tickets gives event managers an overview of reserved and available places in JEM 5.1.0. It shows programmes, subevents, rooms and areas in an expandable table. The view is available in both the administrator area and the frontend.

Tickets also provides a private reservation PDF with QR, manual payment confirmation and quantity-based entrance control. Scanning a QR only looks up the reservation; recording payment and recording entry are separate, explicit actions.

Open Tickets

  • Backend: open Components → JEM → Control Panel. In the Data section, click Tickets, immediately after Registration.
  • Generated frontend menu: sign in and open JEM → Management → Tickets.
  • Your own frontend menu: create a Joomla menu item and select JEM → Tickets as its Menu Item Type. Choose its access level and configure the options described below.

Create JEM Menu includes Tickets under Management as a direct link. The generated entry uses the Special access level. Running the generator again adds or updates this entry without creating another copy.

Users need access to the menu as well as the relevant JEM permissions. Backend access requires permission to access JEM and either Manage Attendees or the relevant Tickets staff permission. In the frontend, signed-in users see events they may edit or serve as authorised ticket staff. Reading movements also requires View Registration History. Making a menu visible does not grant these permissions.

Start with an overview

  1. Open Tickets. By default, it shows Published events under Current and upcoming.
  2. Select a period and, if needed, a category, venue or location.
  3. Enter part of an event title and click Apply / Refresh. Changing a visible dropdown or date control applies that selection automatically.
  4. Use the expand buttons beside programme and room rows, or choose Expand all or Collapse all.
  5. Check Updated to see when the displayed figures were loaded. Click Apply / Refresh to load them again.

The default period uses calendar dates: it includes events ending today or later, including events that started on an earlier date and continue into the selected period. It does not exclude an event merely because its finishing time today has passed.

Use All dates to include past and undated events. The other filters still apply: choosing All dates does not automatically include archived or unpublished events.

Dates and navigation arrows

The operational table uses short dates and times, for example 10/11/2026 and 09:30, leaving room for the counters. Navigation arrows have larger symbols and descriptive tooltips.

Today always selects the current day. Day uses the selected date, or today if no valid date is selected. Week, Month and Year show the period containing the selected date; the date box displays the start of that period. Today follows Joomla's configured timezone, and weeks follow JEM's configured first day of the week.

Combine the filters

  • Event title, Category and Venue: narrow the report to the events you need.
  • Country → State / Region → County → City: each selected location narrows the choices below it. With only a country selected, City can offer cities across that country. Changing a parent selection clears the visible child selections.
  • Publication: choose Published, Unpublished, Archived, Trashed or All.
  • Event status: choose Scheduled, Cancelled, Postponed, Rescheduled, Moved online or All.

Location choices come from JEM events and venues within your permitted event scope. They are not a complete directory of countries or towns. An online event without a venue can appear when no location filter excludes it.

Publication and Event status describe the event. The Confirmed, Waiting and Invited columns describe its bookings. For example, a published but cancelled event can retain confirmed reservations that still need attention; viewing it in Tickets does not change those reservations.

Clear restores the menu's configured defaults. A fixed country or other default may therefore remain selected. In the backend, Clear returns to the standard report defaults.

Read the hierarchy and counters

A programme can contain subevents with their own dates, times and venues. Where configured, an event expands into rooms and areas. If the parent accepts its own registrations, these appear under Programme registration.

A permitted parent may appear as Context only when its children match the filters but the parent does not. Its own bookings then do not contribute to the summary. Programme totals include only matching events. Pagination keeps each programme group together; room and area rows are breakdowns of their event, not extra bookings.

ColumnMeaning
CapacityThe applicable configured place limit for the event or area.
ReservedPlaces held back by the organiser. These reduce free capacity.
ConfirmedPlaces in confirmed registrations. This does not mean they have been paid for or used to enter.
WaitingPlaces requested on the waiting list.
InvitedPlaces in invitations. This optional column is hidden by default.
FreeRemaining capacity after applicable limits, confirmed places and organiser holds. Registration conditions still determine whether a new booking is allowed.
PaidConfirmed places covered by a recorded payment. Free reservations are not counted as paid.
EnteredCumulative admitted places, including previous entries if a reservation is subsequently cancelled. This is not current occupancy; exits are not tracked.
CancelledRecorded cancellation quantities, including supported partial reductions. This is a history total, not a count of occupied places or refunds.

All booking figures count places, not orders or unique people. The same person booked into two subevents contributes to both subevents. Programme capacities and free places are not added across independent events.

∞ means no capacity limit is configured. — means a value is not applicable or is unavailable; it does not mean zero. Cancelled places do not occupy capacity, and must not be added to free capacity a second time.

Click a linked Confirmed, Waiting or Invited count to open the event's attendee list with that status selected. A linked cancellation count opens its recorded movements when you have history permission. The Movements panel shows previous and new booking states and quantities; older or imported records may have incomplete history, and area-level cancellation detail may be unavailable.

Examples

A programme with two sessions. A conference has 10 places booked directly against the parent, 30 in its morning workshop and 20 in its afternoon workshop. With all three events matching, the programme shows 60 confirmed places. This does not mean 60 different visitors. Filtering to the morning workshop excludes the other bookings from that summary.

Areas sharing an event limit. An event allows 50 places, has 40 confirmed and holds 2 for the organiser: 8 remain free. Two areas may each have physical room for 15 more people, but neither can exceed the shared remainder of 8. Adding their displayed free capacities would overstate availability.

A partial cancellation. Reducing a booking from 4 places to 2 records 2 cancelled places. Cancelling the remaining 2 adds another 2. The history then shows 4 cancelled places and the booking occupies no capacity. The historical original quantity is not an active reservation.

Review archived events. Select All dates and set Publication to Archived. Add a title, venue or category filter if needed. Choosing All dates alone would keep the default Published filter.

Configure a frontend menu item

Open the Joomla menu item of type Tickets. Its settings are grouped as follows:

For example, set Default country to Spain and hide the country filter to create a Spain-specific report. You can also hide the Country table column to leave more space for event names. The report remains subject to each user's event permissions.

Automatic refresh waits while the browser tab is hidden or focus is inside the filter form. It reloads the current report; it is not a live payment or entrance monitor. On smaller screens, filters wrap and the table can scroll horizontally.

If an event is missing

  1. Check the period. Past and undated events require an appropriate period or All dates.
  2. Check Publication and Event status independently.
  3. Clear temporary filters, then check the menu defaults, including any hidden fixed filters.
  4. Check that the signed-in user has the required event permissions.
  5. Refresh after changing bookings elsewhere.

Reservation PDF and QR

JEM Mailer attaches a private reservation PDF to the owner's confirmed booking email. Waiting-list, invited and cancelled reservations do not receive an admission PDF. The owner can also open their registration details and choose Download reservation PDF. Authorised desk staff can download it after looking up the reservation.

The QR contains the opaque reservation locator. It contains no personal information, payment status or password. It stays the same after payment and partial admission. An old PDF never overrides the current state stored in JEM. Keep the document private. It is a reservation document, not a legal invoice or proof that a payment was received.

Set up staff permissions

  • Tickets: confirm payment allows a cashier to record an actual external collection.
  • Tickets: record admission allows a door controller to admit eligible places.
  • Assign these permissions in JEM component permissions, or restrict them through event category permissions. Staff must also have access to the chosen frontend menu. Backend staff additionally need access to the Joomla administrator area and JEM.
  • Event editing and attendee management do not automatically grant either new permission. A controller does not need event-edit permission and cannot confirm payments unless separately authorised.

Look up a reservation

  1. Open Tickets in the frontend or backend and click Read QR / Ticket desk.
  2. Select the event being served at this desk or entrance. A locator from another event is rejected.
  3. Enter the locator and choose Find reservation, or use a keyboard-style QR reader. To use a camera, click Read QR with camera and allow camera access.
  4. If several cameras are available, select the desired camera from the list in the dialog. Camera access requires HTTPS or localhost and browser permission. Images are decoded on the device; JEM does not upload camera images.
  5. Check the event, reservation holder, places, payment and remaining entry quantities. Merely looking up or scanning a QR does not record payment or entry.

Confirm payment at the ticket desk

  1. Receive the cash, or verify that the bank transfer has actually arrived.
  2. Enter the full outstanding amount in the displayed currency. Select Cash at the ticket desk or Verified bank transfer. A transfer requires a bank reference; a cash receipt reference is optional.
  3. Click Confirm payment received, review the event, locator and amount, and confirm the popup. Cancel leaves the reservation unchanged.
  4. JEM records the amount, method, reference, operator and UTC time. It does not charge a card or initiate a bank transfer.

The current desk records the complete outstanding balance, not instalments. A priced order with a missing amount cannot be treated as free. An event without a stored price does not require payment in JEM; do not use a free booking to represent an unrecorded external ticket price.

Confirm entry

  1. Look up the reservation at the selected event entrance.
  2. The reservation must be confirmed, the event must be published and neither Cancelled nor Postponed, and the order must be paid or require no payment.
  3. Select the area or admission group, then enter the number of people actually entering now. The initial quantity is one, not the entire group.
  4. Click Confirm entry, review the summary and confirm the popup. JEM rechecks the current state immediately before recording entry.
  5. The remaining quantity decreases. Two simultaneous readers cannot admit the same last place twice. Retrying the same submitted operation does not create another entry.

Example: a reservation has three places and an order total of EUR 30.00. Before collection, entry is blocked. The cashier records EUR 30.00. Two people arrive first: the controller admits two, leaving one available. Later the same QR admits the third person. A further scan shows that all places have been used and cannot admit another person.

Areas: a booking with two places in Area A and two in Area B has separate remaining quantities. Admitting two in Area A does not consume Area B's places or allow a third person into Area A.

Cancellation and audit

The existing cancellation rules still apply. A permitted cancellation releases booking capacity immediately and blocks further use of the cancelled reservation. It does not erase previous payment or admission records. A free or unpaid cancellation needs no refund. If a payment was recorded, JEM shows the amount awaiting an external refund; it does not claim to have refunded the money.

A cancelled paid reservation cannot be reactivated by reusing its old payment. An order awaiting an external refund cannot be increased against those funds either. These cases require administrative settlement review; this version does not record completed refunds. A paid marker without its payment audit is also rejected for review.

The dialog's recent history shows the last 20 payments and entries with the operator ID and UTC time. The full append-only operations ledger remains stored, including after ordinary reservation or event deletion. Explicit removal of all JEM data removes this history too.

Entered counts admissions, not people currently inside. Exit tracking, re-entry, payment gateways, refund execution and a turnstile API are not part of this implementation. The same reservation QR can be presented again only while unused eligible places remain.
